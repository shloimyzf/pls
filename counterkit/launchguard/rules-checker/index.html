What it checks

Detectable from your rules text

  • 1 · Open rules — allow read, write: if true, and test-mode rules with a 30-day fuse
  • 2 · “Signed in” as “authorised” — request.auth != null with no ownership or role check behind it
  • 3 · Roles in user docs — a rule trusting .data.role from a Firestore document
  • 4 · No field validation — write allows with no hasOnly / hasAll allowlist anywhere
  • 5 · Client-set IDs & timestamps — ownerId / authorId / createdAt accepted without == request.auth.uid / == request.time
  • 6 · Orders writable by clients — an orders block clients can update (where status: “paid” would live)
  • 7 · Client-supplied prices — price / total mentioned in rules instead of recomputed server-side
  • 8 · Storage wide open — only when you paste storage.rules too

Not detectable from rules text

These three holes live outside the rules file, so the checker can only point you at them:

  • 9 · No App Check — your API is everyone’s API without it
  • 10 · Secrets in the client or repo — grep your bundle and your git history for service-account keys
  • 11 · No backups, alerts, or monitoring — the first incident becomes unrecoverable without them

All three are in the free starter’s launch checklist. Two good signs the checker also reports: a catch-all deny (if false) and real ownership checks (request.auth.uid ==).
